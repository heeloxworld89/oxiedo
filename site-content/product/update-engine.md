01

### The problem

A deployed medical AI model is actively decaying in the field. Reapproval of a new model costs more than the original development. So it stays deployed, degrading, until it fails an audit. The FDA's Predetermined Change Control Plan (PCCP) framework creates an approved path — but only if the model's changes can be declared, bounded, and evidenced in advance.

02

### What it delivers

Supervises model retraining strictly inside a declared, cryptographically signed bound. Produces a continuous, immutable record demonstrating that every internal modification stayed inside the declared regulatory limit. Built precisely to the FDA PCCP framework specifications.

03

### Who it is for

A fit for

- Regulated clinical AI deployers facing model decay with no viable change-control path

Primary territory: Europe first (EU AI Act, Health Data Space mandate March 2029).

04

### How it works

The Update Engine is built from 3 of the six capabilities of the ORMAS architecture. Each is a property of how the network trains, not a tool bolted on afterwards.

1. 01 — ACCOUNT — Emit a bounded, timestamped, causally attributed record of every modification the network made to itself.

2. 02 — PROTECT — Gate a component out of the forward path when its health falls, so a failing region cannot poison what follows.

3. 03 — REPAIR — Apply a bounded, conserved, logged correction to a named component under a declared per-diagnosis bound.

On customer infrastructure. The licence is on-premise and per-institution: nothing is sent to a third party, there is no hosted tier, and no usage metering phones home. In a hospital enclave or a controlled environment, that is a requirement rather than a preference.
