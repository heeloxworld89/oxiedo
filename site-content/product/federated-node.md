01

### The problem

Federated training destroys attribution. By the time the shared model aggregates at the central server, no participating party can say what their specific data contributed to the global model — or prove they have the ability to remove it.

02

### What it delivers

Runs The Separator across N participating parties. Only the shared, invariant knowledge aggregates. Each party's source-specific capacity stays local, securely named, bounded, and completely removable. Provides a definitive per-party attribution record before aggregation occurs.

04

### How it works

The Federated Node is built from 4 of the six capabilities of the ORMAS architecture. Each is a property of how the network trains, not a tool bolted on afterwards.

1. 01 — ACCOUNT — Emit a bounded, timestamped, causally attributed record of every modification the network made to itself.

2. 02 — DETECT — Identify which samples are damaging which component, and separate mislabelled from hard-but-correct.

3. 03 — ISOLATE — Allocate dedicated capacity bound to a named sample set, under a bound, with the binding recorded.

4. 04 — SUPPRESS — Remove an isolated component and ship without it, with a bounded statement about what changed.

No. ORMAS is one product under one licence, and The Federated Node is one of the seven things it is proven at. An institution turns on what its situation needs; there is no separate purchase, no separate integration and no separate contract.

#### Does this only work in my industry?

The mechanism does not know what industry it is in. What changes between them is the noun, not the method. The table above is the same mechanism with each sector's own vocabulary substituted, which is why one product serves all of them.

#### Where does it run, and does our data leave?

On customer infrastructure. The licence is on-premise and per-institution: nothing is sent to a third party, there is no hosted tier, and no usage metering phones home. In a hospital enclave or a controlled environment, that is a requirement rather than a preference.
